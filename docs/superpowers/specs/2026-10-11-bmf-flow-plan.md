# BMF — flow and variety: the shot plan

**Status: draft for the user's approval** (2026-10-11). Nothing here is built until it's approved.
The user's transition notes (timestamps from the preview) get folded in section by section.

## Why it's samey

The spec (§3) planned **a set-piece per section**, its lyrics on surfaces chosen for what the lines say:
the speed sign for "Going too fast", a walk signal whose figure crip-walks, a MR HANDLE IT vanity plate,
the gear shift's **D** for "Put it in drive". M2 built something else: **one generic system**. Every
phrase goes on the next kind of sign in a rotation (road paint, wall, billboard, sign…), the camera
looks at it, and then it goes back to the car. That is 121 phrases with one grammar. Changing the kind
of sign doesn't change the loop, so the video feels samey even though no two signs repeat.

Two more causes:

- **The edit follows the syllables, not the music.** There's a shot per phrase, so the cutting rate tracks
  how fast she sings. The line takes (2026-10-10) halved the cuts, but the grammar is still per phrase.
- **Sections don't hand over.** The road just continues from one section into the next. Only the tunnel
  has a designed way in and out, and it's the one section you said feels different.

## Principles

1. **Each section is a set-piece**: its own place, its own lyric surfaces chosen for its lines, its own
   camera and editing rhythm. No rotation of sign kinds. The carrier kinds become a toolkit that each
   set-piece draws from.
2. **Choruses rhyme and verses don't.** Every chorus runs the same four-beat ritual, bigger each time:
   his campaign → the place he's from → "dropping" → "fly" in the air. The verses are three different
   films.
3. **Cut to the music.** Cut on bar lines, at most one cut per line, and one take inside a line. Each
   section change gets a designed transition. Target: about 30 cuts in the film (52 now, 105 two days ago).
4. **A small transition vocabulary**, nothing else:
   - **Cut** on a downbeat.
   - **Follow**: the camera stays with the car and the lyric comes to it.
   - **Crane**: up or down, including down into the open-top car and back out.
   - **Whip**: a blurred pan of 0.25 s or less, between neighbouring surfaces.
   - **Push-through**: into the tunnel's mouth, or through a sign.

   The swoops and pose-to-pose glides go.
5. **The tenets stay**: every lyric legible while sung (the guard), nothing read ahead, left to right,
   physical over fades, no cards over the picture.

### 1 · Chorus 1 — 0:16–0:31 · the boulevard, golden hour · M
The ritual, first and smallest:

### 2 · Verse 1 — 0:31–1:01 · city streets, stop-and-go · L — **built 2026-10-11, in review**
**The grammar: inside the car at the lights.** At each red light, a held shot through the windshield,
with the two of them in the foreground. The lyric is on whatever is out there. When the light turns
green, one cut.
- **"Going too fast…"**: a speed-feedback sign flashing (YOUR SPEED → the line).
- **"…lies you tell yourself"**: a bus pulls across and its side ad carries the line.
- **"…don't play yourself"**: the crosswalk signal's hand.
- **"I'm not the one…"**: the plate and bumper stickers of the car ahead at the red.
- **"You say you feel different…"**: the car's radio display, an insert inside.
- **"Compliment my energy…" / "…pull up and get to dumping"**: a drive-thru menu board, then its
  PULL UP sign.
- **"…with all the ruckus, can you?"**: the receipt printing.
- **Edit**: the lights set the rhythm. Hold on red, cut on green.
- **Out**: the on-ramp. The car accelerates and the camera *cranes* out of the car up to the high chase.

### 3 · Chorus 2 — 1:01–1:16 · the freeway · M

- **Out**: the SLAUSON AVE exit gantry. The car takes the off-ramp and drops down into the neighbourhood.

### 4 · Verse 2 — 1:16–1:46 · Slauson, the neighbourhood · L
**The grammar: low and slow, lowrider culture.** The camera is at wheel height and swings round the car
at the corners. The lyrics are painted by hand: murals, store windows, a sign painter's lettering. This
is a new type voice for the film.
- **"Ooh, he crip walkin'"**: the walk signal's figure crip-walks, and the car three-wheels (built).
- **"Took me to Slauson…"**: a SLAUSON street sign on the corner.
- **"…call him Mr. Handle It"**: a vanity plate on a parked lowrider.
- **"…them chickens you been trickin' on"**: a chicken shack's rooftop sign.
- **"…your ride or die"**: a car wash's sign.
- **"…good in every hood" and the rest**: murals.
- **Out**: a turn at the end of the street opens onto the sea: *whip* to the coast.

### 5 · Chorus 3 — 1:46–2:01 · the coast road, sunset · M

### 9 · Outro — 2:46–3:02 · the overlook, parked · M
Stillness: the camera barely moves, and the bills drift.
- **"…we might crash"**: the city's lights go out below, block by block. This calls back to the
  tunnel's lamps dying, and it's the in-world crash in place of the card.
- **"Tryna make it last"**: their tail lights are the last thing lit.
- **Then**: the motel's sign as the end card (built).

## Build notes

**Verse 1 (2026-10-11), second version.** Built as `app/src/drive/sets/verse1.ts` (the drive model) and
`app/src/scenes/sets/verse1.ts` (the props).

The first version stopped the car at a red light and a drive-thru. You liked the creativity but not the stops: "I don't
like the car stopping. I feel like that really ruins the flow." So the car now never stops: it rushes past the speed
trailer at 16 m/s, then cruises at 11 m/s. Its lyrics ride on what it follows and what's in it, or stand where the
camera watches it go by:

- **"Going too fast…"**: the speed trailer, as before.
- **"…lies you tell yourself"**: the tail ad of the bus they tailgate, seen from the back seat.
- **"You kinda cute…"**: her phone, the texts she's sending as message bubbles.
- **"I'm not the one…"**: the bus turns off at a cross street (its light green), revealing the van ahead with
  stickers on its back doors and MY MAN on its plate, seen from over the hood.
- **"You say you feel different…"**: the radio, as before.
- **"Compliment my energy…"**: a burger stand's menu board, the car passing it.
- **"All I ask…"**: the stand's marquee, up on its pole.
- **"…can you?"**: back on her phone.

**Edit.** Four cuts in thirty seconds (into the trailer, onto the bus, onto the stand, back to the phone), plus the cut
into chorus 2; whips between the rest.

**Fixes found on the way.**

- Lines now belong to their own section: "Going too fast" had fallen into chorus 1 by 0.02 s.
- The town around the faces had all but emptied: 17 buildings in the film, before this change. Verse 1 now has its
  own low-rise blocks along its street, and chorus 2's faces start past them.
- Set-piece panels carry their backings as solids, so the guard sees them. Without that, a shot looked straight into
  the menu board's back.
